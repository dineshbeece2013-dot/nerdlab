Terraform: Changing What Already Exists

The part that makes people nervous

Creating infrastructure from nothing is the easy half. The half that matters is changing something that is already running and carrying real traffic.

Terraform keeps a record of everything it has built. When you change the file, it compares what you asked for against that record and tells you — before touching anything — exactly what it intends to do. Four things can happen to a resource: it can be added, changed in place, destroyed and rebuilt, or destroyed.

The third one is the one that catches people out. Some settings can be adjusted on a running machine; others cannot, and asking for them means the machine is thrown away and a new one built. Terraform tells you which — if you read the plan.
